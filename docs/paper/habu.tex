\begin{abstract}
Writing custom GPU kernels remains an expert activity in which most errors
surface only at runtime. We present Habu, a self-hosted checked Forth whose
compiler types kernel compositions statically, and Maki, a model-development
layer built on it that treats GPU compilation as computer-aided design:
models are captured into an IR, fused by a planner with exact byte
accounting and typed legality rules, lowered to generated PTX kernels, and
\emph{verified} --- every generated kernel is proven against a checked host
reference on the target device before its artifact is promoted. The same IR
that executes also differentiates: adjoints are generated, gradient-checked,
and used to train a model from scratch through the identical pipeline. The
toolchain is a single self-hosted binary under 128\,KB. On a Jetson Orin NX
we measure parity with hand-written Triton at the streaming-memory roof and
show that the checked target shifts the stack-discipline error class from
runtime to author time. \todo{Revise abstract once compute-bound results
land (\S\ref{sec:perf}).}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The deep learning compilation landscape divides labor in a familiar way:
vendor libraries cover common operations; kernel DSLs such as Triton
\cite{triton} let experts write the rest; graph compilers fuse and schedule
above them. Two costs are accepted as normal. First, kernel authorship is
unchecked: a Triton author who mis-tracks a mask or an offset discovers it
at runtime, if at all. Second, the artifacts of compilation are
\emph{emitted, not proven}: nothing in the toolchain certifies that the
fused kernel computes what the unfused graph did.

Habu rejects both costs. It is a Forth in the lineage of Chuck Moore's
OKAD~\cite{okad}: a small, self-hosted system (the complete engine,
compiler, and checker fit in a binary under 128\,KB) in which programs are
compositions of words, and---the departure from tradition---every word
carries a typed, row-polymorphic stack effect that the self-hosted checker
verifies. Kernels for the GPU are written (or generated) in the same
checked language and emitted as PTX text.

Maki, the model layer, treats the remaining pipeline as computer-aided
design rather than black-box compilation. A model is captured into an IR by
a checked definition (\code{MODEL:}); a fusion planner groups nodes into
regions under typed legality rules, reporting exact traffic in bytes; a
memory planner reports coalescing classes; a schedule layer names candidate
kernels per region family; and a lowering layer generates one PTX kernel
per region. At every stage the system emits facts a person (or an agent)
can audit, and the final artifact is admitted only through gates:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CERTIFY} --- static legality of the whole plan;
\item \textbf{GOLDEN} --- the generated device kernels reproduce a checked
  host executor's results on the target hardware, element-wise under
  per-class tolerances (or match an external reference artifact);
\item \textbf{GRADCHECK} --- generated adjoints agree with central
  finite differences;
\item \textbf{PROFILE} --- measured occupancy and roofline placement
  (mandatory to run, non-blocking).
\end{itemize}
Only a model that passes is \emph{promoted}: its schedule selection and
evidence are recorded in a content-addressed store keyed by region
signature, shape class, dtype, layout, alignment, target, and engine build.

This paper contributes: (1) the design of a checked concatenative kernel
target and the measurement that it shifts a whole error class to author
time (\S\ref{sec:errors}); (2) Model CAD --- a verification-first
compilation pipeline whose every generated kernel is device-proven against
a checked reference (\S\ref{sec:cad}); (3) a verified-gradient facility in
which the training IR and the gradchecked IR are the same object,
demonstrated by training a model from scratch through the pipeline
(\S\ref{sec:train}); and (4) measurements on Jetson Orin NX
(\S\ref{sec:results}). \todo{Add contribution (5): compute-bound

\section{Background and Related Work}\label{sec:related}
Tillet et al.'s Triton paper~\cite{triton} classifies prior systems as
tensor-level IRs (XLA, Glow: pattern-matched templates), polyhedral
compilers (Tensor Comprehensions, Diesel: affine loop nests), and loop
synthesizers (Halide, TVM: user-specified schedules), positioning Triton as
tile-level operations inside a traditional pipeline with
compiler-owned parallelization. Habu shares Triton's durable insight ---
the author writes a scalar-looking program over tiles; the compiler owns
intra-tile concurrency --- but differs on three axes. First, the
composition is \emph{typed}: fused chains are checked words whose stack
effects the compiler verifies, so a malformed composition fails to compile
rather than misbehaving on device. Second, fusion is a \emph{planner
decision with an audit trail} (regions, split reasons, byte deltas), not an
authoring convention. Third, artifacts are \emph{gated}: device-vs-host
verification is part of compilation, not an external test the author may
skip. Modern Triton (Python DSL on MLIR) retains per-kernel horizons and
per-process JIT autotuning; Habu's tuning is persistent and content-keyed.

\section{Error-Class Measurement}\label{sec:errors}
On identical tasks against real Triton 3.5.1 on the same device, a
six-candidate error battery shows the checked target catching
stack-discipline and composition errors at author time with located
diagnostics and zero GPU involvement, where Triton surfaces them at runtime
or not at all; authoring studies (pass@1 = 3/3 both targets on a fused
chain) show reachability is comparable, so the difference is \emph{where
bugs are caught}, not what experts can express.

\section{Limitations}
V1 caps are explicit and fail closed: one contraction per region;
$K\le 256$; elementwise prologues into contractions are planned but not yet
lowered; broadcast operands and multi-output regions are staged for the
OPTIMIZE wiring; PROFILE's roofs await device microbenchmarks. The checker
types stack discipline and roles, not semantics --- numeric correctness is
the golden gate's job, by design. \todo{Refresh against the dot state at
