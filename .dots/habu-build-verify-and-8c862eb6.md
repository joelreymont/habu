Problem: tier 1 compiles a trivial word as three immutable modules (HIR, selection, and a combine rebuild whenever the body carries a combinable pair), and every module is built through a fresh builder, frozen, run through the freeze verifier and canonically digested (docs/compiler-ir-design.md: FREEZE consumes the builder, validates the complete module, computes its canonical digest). The profile (compile-floor lane, 2026-09-16, 3dca9e49) prices a module rebuild at about 230 us on a two-op body; the memory machinery (registry, clones) is the visible part, the design cost is the rest: a full validation and digest per pass per word, and a rebuild instead of an in-place rewrite because modules are immutable. Joel: there is a lot of inefficiency here apart from memory management. Acceptance: (1) a per-phase inclusive table for the trivial word and the corpus, from the internal profiler, committed in docs/compiler-measurements.md: HIR build, freeze verify, digest, selection, register allocation, combine (and its rebuild), emit, publish; (2) the combine pass rewrites the selection module before its freeze instead of building a fourth module, or the passes share one builder per word with one freeze at the end; (3) the canonical digest is computed only when a consumer asks for it (the fixpoint certification, artifact identity, the frozen cache) and never on the JIT path of an ordinary word; (4) the freeze verifier runs once per word on the module that is emitted, its strength unchanged, and the per-pass verifications that re-check the same invariants are removed with the reasoning; (5) compile-floor and corpus numbers before and after each step with tools/tier-bench.f, byte fixpoint, full gate green.
