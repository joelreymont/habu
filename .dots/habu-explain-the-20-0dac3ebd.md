Full context: measured by tools/codegen-workload.f, row scan-floor. Two byte-identical drivers (WORKLOAD:SCAN-OLD and WORKLOAD:SCAN-CTL-A, 324 bytes each, one call instruction each) calling two byte-identical 144-byte engine-compiled copies of the same body (HOT-ENGINE:FOLD-C and HOT-FIXED:FOLD-C, published seconds apart into different packages from ONE source string) differ by 18 to 35 per cent across every run, reproducibly, with the slower side always the one that reaches HOT-ENGINE. Fresh drivers compiled at the end of the run and interleaved reproduce it, so it is not driver layout and not measurement order: it tracks the CALLEE's publication. The workload's shape is one call per byte over a 4 KiB buffer, so the effect is per-call. Consequence: for that shape the code generator's own effect (SYM-FOLD-C is 1.26 ns of routine old against 0.81 ns new, per tools/codegen-compare.f) is an order of magnitude below the layout effect and the row reports NOT MEASURABLE. Work: find the mechanism with a tool - publish the same body at a controlled sequence of addresses and time a driver against each, so the report can say whether it is 64-byte line straddling, a fetch-window boundary, or branch-target aliasing - and decide whether the publication seam should align a routine's entry. That is a real code-quality question: an aligner would raise every hot leaf call in the system by the same 20 per cent. Acceptance: an alignment sweep in tools/, its numbers, and either a named mechanism or a measured refutation. Update 2026-08-03 (destruction review of the workload tool): this is not only a layout question — it is a cross-tool disagreement about the chain's emitted leaf. tools/codegen-compare.f says FOLD-C new is 0.82 ns/call; the workload's per-call derivation says 3.13 ns/call for the byte-identical 72-byte routine — 3.8x apart — while both tools agree on the old arm (1.18 vs 1.28). Averaged over four independent chain placements the chain arm was ~18 percent slower than the engine arm on this shape, and the FASTEST chain placement was still slower than the fastest engine placement, so a real chain-side effect likely hides under the placement noise. The alignment sweep must therefore also time engine-published vs chain-published copies of the same body across the address sweep, so the answer separates 'where the routine landed' from 'how the chain published it'.  Findings 2026-08-04 (explain-20): measured by tools/codegen-align-sweep.f, written up in docs/codegen-placement.md, two full runs. (a) MECHANISM: the effect is a function of the CALLEE entry address modulo 64 and of nothing else swept. Period 64, not the hosts 128-byte line (Apple M2 Max, hw.cachelinesize 128): both 128-byte halves behave identically. Engine fold 2108/2233 ps/call at its best offset against 4340/4210 in a contiguous bad window at entry mod 64 in 20..36 - a factor of 2.06 and 1.89 - against a replicate floor (arms steered to the SAME residue at different addresses) of 76 to 90 parts per thousand. REFUTED by measurement: call-site alignment (whole driver swept across the period, span 1.26x, no band, phase 2); page crossing including an arm whose body really crosses a 16 KiB boundary (7.5 to 9.5 per cent, phase 4); and EVERY front-end reading - fetch block, decode window, iTLB - by phase 5, where a BRANCHLESS body of exactly the folds 144 bytes, published under the same name and reached by the same driver text, is flat to 2.8 and 9.5 parts per thousand with no band anywhere. What survives is the callees own branch instruction addresses: address-indexed branch-prediction state with a 64-byte period, where one window of entry offsets collides the routines own branches. Denser branches, wider window: engine 4 branches in 148 bytes and 5 of 16 offsets bad, chain 6 in 76 bytes and 10 of 16 bad, branchless 0 and none. (b) THE CHAIN LEAF IS REALLY SLOWER: at each bodys own best placement, chain 2704/2855 against engine 2108/2233 ps/call - 28 per cent, both runs. Cause read off the two instruction streams: the engine emits cmp/cset/neg/cbz per test, the chain emits cmp plus a b.cond to the then-arm plus a b to the else-arm plus a b to the join - half the instructions, half again the branches, three times the branch density. Filed as dot habu-select-instead-of-2f51e91c. The 3.8x cross-tool disagreement is NOT a placement effect and not an error: tools/codegen-compare-cases2.f times the fold on a CONSTANT byte, so its branches never mispredict; filed as dot habu-say-that-codegen-697b629f. (c) AN ENTRY ALIGNER IN NPUB IS NOT WORTH IT - NO. A fixed aligner puts every routine at one residue, and the sign of a residue is a property of the BODY: residue 0 costs the engine fold 2341/2233 against a period mean of 3067/3029 (a 24 to 26 per cent win) and the chain fold 5004/4605 against 4274/4194 (a 10 to 17 per cent loss). Same rule, same host, same run, opposite signs. No leaf count turns that around, because the count multiplies a per-leaf expectation that is not positive; and the effect exists only for leaves with data-dependent branches, while a leaf branchless enough to be immune is usually small enough that the engine copies it into its caller and there is no call left to speed up. The measured recommendation is to remove branches (dot habu-select-instead-of-2f51e91c), which improves every placement at once, rather than to move them.
