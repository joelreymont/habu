Full context: found while adding operand bounds to the shipped assembler (dots habu-refuse-out-of-3536f1ed and habu-guard-x18-in-7cc74c05). src/arch/arm64/asm.f, src/arch/arm64/icode.f and src/arch/arm64/mnem.f contain no package at all: every encoder, mnemonic and label word they define is a global name. tools/srclist.f puts the three files third, fourth and fifth in the engine source prefix, ahead of src/habu/habu1.f which is where packages come from, and every later prefix file resolves their names bare - src/habu/jit.f, src/habu/habu1.f and src/habu/habu2.f call LDR,, CBZ,, MOVZHW and the rest with no qualifier, as do tools/asm-src-test.f, test/icode-fixup-test.f and test/compiler/insn-schema.f. Because of that, tools/package-diff-lint-core.f now carries the three paths in GLOBAL-IMPLEMENTATION? as an interim entry, on the same terms as src/core/checker.f and src/core/render.f; measured on 2026-07-30, without it adding the operand bounds to asm.f reported ten ownership faults for words the encoders already published, so the gate rejected every possible change to the ARM64 assembler. Fix: give the three files real package owners (a package for the encoders, one for the label and branch layer, one for the mnemonic layer, or one shared owner if that is what the layering really is), migrate every caller to qualified calls with no forwarding shims or aliases, and delete the three entries from the lint's allowlist together with the TEST-ARM64-EXEMPTION fixtures that pin them. Two things have to be established first: whether the Gforth recovery compiler in tools/bootstrap.sh can read a package declaration in these files at all - their headers say they are written in the standalone's Forth precisely because it has to read them before the native checker exists - and what the packaging does to the engine fixpoint census and binary size. Related: habu-give-the-standalone-8cc02435 wants checked stack effects on the same three files, and doing both at once is likely cheaper than doing them apart.
