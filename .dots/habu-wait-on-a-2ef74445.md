## Design (lead, 2026-11-11, read src/lib/process.f:285-350, :655-682)

One public word in PROCESS beside POLL-IN-OR-TIMEOUT: `( fd fd ms -- count )`, the second descriptor the cancel. It arms slots 0 and 1 (PFD-AT!), waits with POLL-RESTART to the deadline as POLL-IN-OR-TIMEOUT does, and reports as a capture does: a negative rc is E-PROC-OUTPUT, the deadline E-PROC-TIMEOUT, the cancel descriptor readable or hung up E-PROC-CANCELLED (CANCEL-HEARD :660-663 is the rule), else the count. No new error code; "which one ended it" is the throw code. The capture's own cancel (CMD:CANCEL-FD!, slot 4) is unchanged: it belongs to a command, and a bare wait has none. Caller: maki src/kernel.f:469 MORE and :665 ENDED switch to it when the service holds the worker (Maki landing 4; the cancel descriptor is the context's, project.f:538). Open with Joel: the word's name (tim's plan: POLL-IN-OR-CANCEL).

Ruling (Joel, 2026-11-11), replacing the Design above. Asked the name of the two-descriptor word: "POLL? and don't you normally wait on at least 2 descriptors in this case, passing both the real one and the cancel one to the underlying OS poll mechanism?". Shown the general shape, he chose "PROCESS:POLL, general": `PROCESS:POLL ( n ms -- count )` waits on the first n slots the caller armed with PFD-AT! until one has an event or the window is over (E-PROC-TIMEOUT; a failed poll E-PROC-OUTPUT), and the caller reads which with PFD-REVENTS. The library does not know which descriptor means cancel: Maki arms the worker's reply in slot 0 and its cancel descriptor in slot 1. Lead's additions: n outside 1..PFD-SLOTS and a slot index outside the table are E-A-BOUNDS (PFD-SLOT checked no index); POLL-IN-OR-TIMEOUT is the one-descriptor case of it. Brief: .out/briefs/add-process-poll.md. Follow-up after the install: the five hand-composed `1 ms ms >MS PROCESS:DEADLINE-AT PROCESS:POLL-RESTART` sites (tools/lsp-test-lib.f:1374, test/gate-signal-test.f:211, test/gate-pool.f:549, test/capture-tree-test.f:78, test/check-signal-test.f:118, test/db/pg-kill-test.f:79) call POLL where their handling of a timeout and a failed poll is POLL's.
