title: Enter and leave a Habu word through the data stack

Design section 7.6 says an externally callable Habu word receives its inputs and publishes its outputs through canonical data-stack slots, and the calling-convention seam that just landed binds the OTHER convention: the C-ABI leaf routines the chain emits today, whose arguments arrive in x0.. and whose result leaves in x0, declared as ordered register lists on the routine contract (A64EFF gpr-arg / gpr-out) and honoured by A64RA and A64RAV. A Habu word needs three things that do not exist yet. (1) The data-stack pointer has to be nameable: it lives in a dedicated register the target contract reserves, no A64IR value stands for it, and none can - the allocator hands out values, and this register it may never hand out. It is a live-in the contract declares and the dialect reaches through a form, the way the frame forms reach the stack pointer by naming it rather than by taking it as an operand. (2) The dialect needs data-stack load and store forms: an entry that reads argument i out of slot i of the caller's stack, and an exit that writes result j into slot j, both against that register, with the memory token that orders them against the frame accesses. (3) The convention declaration has to be able to say 'argument i arrives in data-stack slot i' rather than 'in register r' - so the ordered list becomes a list of PLACES, of which a register is one kind and a data-stack slot another. Acceptance: a word compiled by the native chain is callable from the engine the way an interpreted one is, with its inputs taken from the data stack and its outputs published there, and the allocator refuses a program that would hand out the data-stack register. Owners: CTARGET (the reserved register), A64IR, A64EFF, A64RA, A64RAV, A64EMIT.
